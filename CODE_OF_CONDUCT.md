# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

Project spaces should be safe, focused, and useful. Participants must:

- treat people with respect, including when reviewing strong technical disagreement;
- criticize ideas and changes, not people;
- accept correction, disclose conflicts, and give credit;
- protect private information found in logs, saves, reports, or contributor profiles; and
- stop an interaction when a participant or maintainer asks for space.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, threats, sexualized attention, discrimination,
deliberate intimidation, doxxing, repeated unwanted contact, impersonation, plagiarism, or
publishing another person's private information. Technical status, experience, nationality,
race, ethnicity, caste, religion, disability, neurotype, body, age, sex, gender identity,
gender expression, and sexual orientation are never grounds for mistreatment.

## Scope

This code applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews, project chat,
playtests, and public representation of the project. It also applies when private conduct has a
direct, credible effect on participant safety in project spaces.

## Reporting

For a private conduct report, open a
[private GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/AussieWarGod/thousand-and-first/security/advisories/new).
Use a neutral title such as `Private conduct report`; include links, dates, witnesses, and the
outcome needed. Do not put sensitive conduct details in a public issue.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to people needed to assess and resolve the report. They will
consider safety, evidence, pattern, severity, conflicts of interest, and requested privacy.
Retaliation against a reporter or witness is itself a violation. A maintainer named in a report
must not decide that report.

## Enforcement

Responses may include clarification, a private warning, content removal, restricted contact,
temporary exclusion, or permanent exclusion. Immediate safety measures may precede a final
decision. Knowingly false reports and bad-faith use of this process may also be addressed, but
an unproven good-faith report is not misconduct.

Maintainers should record the reason and scope of enforcement privately, tell affected parties
what can safely be disclosed, and revisit temporary measures on their stated date.
